# Labels across languages and markets

Read when labels will be translated or shown to more than one market.

- **Translate for meaning, not word for word,** and re-test the labels with people in that market. A label that passes in one language has proved nothing about another.
- **Some categories do not travel:** seasons, holidays, units, business conventions. Check that the structure still makes sense in each market, not just the words.
- **Keep one canonical structure across locales** where you can, so content and redirects stay manageable. If a market needs a different label, change the label before you change the tree.
- **Give translators the context:** what is behind the label, its siblings, and how long it may be. A label translated from a list, with no destination, is a guess.
- **Leave room.** Translated labels often run longer than the original, so a menu that only just fits in one language may not fit in another.
- **Do not build labels from fragments.** Word order changes between languages, so a label assembled from "Your" plus a variable plus "settings" will break.
- **Treat terms of art with care.** An English term of art may be used as is in one market and translated in another. Ask, or read that market's search logs, rather than assuming.
- **Watch for local trigger words.** The search vocabulary in each market is its own evidence. Do not carry one market's synonyms into another.

Locale in the URL is a separate decision: hand that to url-structure.
