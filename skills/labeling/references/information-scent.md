# Information scent

Read when the labels look fine on their own but people still bounce, or when writing link text and previews.

People follow cues that suggest they are getting closer to what they want, the way an animal follows a scent. Strong scent comes from:

- **Trigger words:** labels that use the words people already have in their heads, ideally the ones they type into search.
- **Specific link text:** "Download the onboarding checklist" rather than "Learn more".
- **Previews:** short descriptions under labels, item counts, sample contents, thumbnails.
- **Breadcrumbs and active states:** confirmation that you are on the path you think you are on.
- **Consistency:** the same thing called the same name everywhere.

Weak scent shows up as pogo-sticking (in, back out, into the next), heavy use of search from navigation pages, and short visits to section pages. Fix the label or the grouping, not the person.

## Scent beats click counts

People abandon a path when the scent goes cold, not when they hit click four. The "three-click rule" is a myth. Dead ends and vague labels are the real cost, so judge each step by whether it clearly smells like the goal.

## When a word cannot carry it

| Situation | Add |
|---|---|
| The label is accurate but broad | A one-line description of what is inside |
| People are unsure the section is worth opening | An item count or two or three sample items |
| Two siblings sound close | A short note on each saying how it differs from the other |
| People arrive deep from search | Breadcrumbs that say where this page lives |

If a label needs a paragraph to explain it, the label or the grouping is wrong.

## Reading the evidence

The signals above need analytics someone has actually collected. If none exist, say so. Do not infer pogo-sticking from a look at the page.

## Judging a set of labels

| 1 (weak) | 3 (sound) | 5 (strong) |
|---|---|---|
| Vague labels such as "Resources" or "Solutions", inconsistent names, or a label that only makes sense inside the company | Clear labels, mostly consistent, a few internal terms remain | A newcomer can predict what is behind every label, and the trigger words match what people search for |
