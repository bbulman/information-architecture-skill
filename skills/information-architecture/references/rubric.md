# Information architecture: rubric

Score a proposal, your own or someone else's, before handing it over. Score each criterion 1 to 5, or mark it N/A when the job doesn't touch it (a menu tweak needs no content model). Quote the evidence for each score.

**Ready to hand over:** an average of 3.5 or more across the scored criteria, and nothing below 2. For anything below 3, say what would raise it.

| # | Criterion | 1 (weak) | 3 (sound) | 5 (strong) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Grounded in evidence** | Built on opinion; no look at what exists | Current structure mapped; some evidence (analytics, search logs, sorts); assumptions stated | Current state counted and audited; research or data behind each major choice; every guess labelled as one |
| 2 | **Matches the users' model** | Org chart or internal jargon | Mostly task or audience based; a few internal terms remain | Structure and labels come from users' own groupings and words |
| 3 | **Taxonomy quality** | Overlapping categories, an "Other" bucket, mixed dimensions in one list | Mostly mutually exclusive; dimensions mostly separated | Each facet is one dimension, mutually exclusive and complete; tested by applying it to the whole corpus |
| 4 | **Depth and breadth** | Very deep, or very wide and unscannable | Reasonable defaults (5 to 9 a level, 3 to 4 deep) with a few exceptions | Shape fits the content size, with each exception justified |
| 5 | **Labels and scent** | Vague labels ("Resources", "Solutions"), inconsistent names | Clear labels, mostly consistent | A newcomer can predict what is behind every label; trigger words match what people search for |
| 6 | **Navigation surfaces** | Everything in one menu | Primary, utility and footer separated | Every surface has an entry rule; contextual navigation and breadcrumbs designed; mobile and tab bar covered |
| 7 | **Homes and paths** | Items in several places, or reachable one way only | One home for most items; search and browse both work | One canonical home per item; browse, search, filters and related links all lead there |
| 8 | **URL rules** | Mixed case, IDs, extensions, no canonical form | Readable, mostly consistent slugs | Written rules for case, slash, hierarchy, locales and slug changes, enforced by the platform |
| 9 | **Content model** | Not considered | Main template types listed | Every URL pattern has a template with its fields and relations; the CMS can enforce slug, parent, status and taxonomy |
| 10 | **Validation plan** | None | Tree test or sort planned | Realistic tasks in users' words, correct answers and success targets defined; no invented results |
| 11 | **Migration** | Old URLs and tags ignored | Redirects listed for the main pages | Every old URL mapped (301 or 410, no chains) and old content re-tagged in the same release |
| 12 | **Scale and governance** | Breaks if content doubles; nobody owns it | Some room to grow; an owner named | Survives ten times the content; term lifecycle, approval path and drift audit defined |

**Common failures to check for first:** an "Other" or "Misc" category; a label that only makes sense inside the company; an item that needs two homes; a primary nav over seven items; a proposal with no "what exists now"; success rates or participant quotes nobody actually collected.
