# Routes, cues and personalisation

Read before adding a cue, a personalised entry point or an AI assistant.

## Several ways in

Browse, search, filters, related links and breadcrumbs each suit a different moment. Some people know what they want and search; others explore. An item should have one canonical home, in the place people expect it, and be reachable from several routes that all lead there. Two homes split search results, links and maintenance.

| 1 (weak) | 3 (sound) | 5 (strong) |
|---|---|---|
| Items in several places, or reachable one way only | One home for most items; search and browse both work | One canonical home per item; browse, search, filters and related links all lead there |

## What makes a good cue

A good cue answers a latent question at the moment of decision. It is:

- **Clear:** understood at a glance.
- **Findable:** where people are already looking.
- **Relevant:** exactly the answer needed.
- **Contextual:** there when needed, quiet otherwise.
- **Obvious:** no instructions needed.
- **Cheap:** costs little attention and little to build.

To find where one is needed, write down the question the person has at each choice point. If the answer is not visible at that point, the cue goes there and not in a help page, a tour or a banner elsewhere.

## Personalisation

Role- or behaviour-based entry points (a "For administrators" door, recently used items, saved views) help, but keep one stable, shared structure underneath. If everyone sees a different menu, people cannot give each other directions, support cannot describe where things are, and the analytics stop meaning anything. Personalise the shortcuts, not the map.

## Before adding AI

Read the support tickets, search log and session notes for what is actually going wrong:

| What you find | What usually fixes it |
|---|---|
| People can't tell what the product can do for them | Visible options, not an empty prompt |
| They pick the wrong one of two similar things | A clearer distinction or a merge |
| The same question arrives again and again | A label, a cue or a page that answers it |
| The task really is open-ended and differs every time | This is where a conversation earns its place |

Buttons and menus do two jobs: they let people act, and they show people what the system can do. An open-ended chat does the first slowly and the second not at all. Use chat where the task really is open-ended; elsewhere, use AI behind a structured interface.

The recurring questions are the useful output of this exercise. Each one that keeps coming back is a candidate for a label, a cue or a page, whether or not an assistant is ever built.
