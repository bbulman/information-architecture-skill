# Running a sort

Read this when choosing cards or writing the instructions.

## Which research method

| Method | Use it to | People |
|---|---|---|
| Open card sort | Discover their categories | 15 to 30 |
| Closed card sort | Test your categories | 15 to 30 |
| Tree test | Validate a hierarchy before building | 20 to 50 |
| Interviews | Understand mental models and vocabulary | 5 to 10 |
| Content audit | Know what exists | 1 or 2 analysts |

For a small site, a quick sort with 8 to 12 people is usually enough to see the dominant pattern. Use the larger numbers when the decision is expensive.

## Selecting cards

Aim for 30 to 60. Past 60, people tire and late placements get careless. Fewer is right when the site has fewer things worth sorting.

| Include | Leave out, and why |
|---|---|
| Items from every section, so no area is missing from the result | Anything with a category word in it ("Billing FAQ" when "Billing" is an option), because it sorts itself |
| The top tasks and the most used items | Numbered or paired series ("Part 1", "Part 2"), which only tell you people can match |
| Items you were unsure how to place | Navigation furniture (Home, Contact, Log in), which has no meaningful group |
| Items at one level of detail | A mix of whole sections and single articles, which forces people to nest |
| One example where many are near-identical | Near-duplicates, which inflate the pair scores without teaching anything |

If a card can only be understood by someone who knows the product's names, describe what it does instead.

## Wording that avoids priming

- Use the plain words a user would use, not the internal name.
- Keep the form consistent, for example all noun phrases or all short tasks. Mixed forms invite grouping by form.
- Reword cards that share a distinctive word. "Shipping rates" and "Shipping delays" will be paired on the word whatever they mean. "What delivery costs" and "Late parcels" test the meaning.
- For a closed or hybrid sort, no card should repeat a word from a category name.
- Keep card text about the same length. A much longer card looks special.

`--check-cards` finds shared words, duplicates, internal codes and category words. It matches words and cannot judge meaning, so read the list yourself as well. Some shared words are unavoidable.

## Instructions

Keep them short and do not name the structure you hope for. For example:

> Please sort these cards into groups that make sense to you. There are no right or wrong answers. Make as many or as few groups as you like, and give each group a name. If a card does not fit anywhere, or you are not sure what it means, leave it unsorted.

For a closed sort, replace the second half with: "Please place each card in the category where you would expect to find it. If it does not fit any of them, leave it unsorted."

Avoid naming example groups or thanking people for agreeing with anything. If the sort is moderated, ask people to think aloud and write down the words they use for each pile. Those words are often worth more than the piles.

## Setting up

- Randomise the card order for every participant.
- Run a pilot on one or two people and fix any card they read differently from how you meant it. Do not include pilot rows in the results if the cards changed.
- Keep participants to one pass. A second sort by the same person is not independent.
