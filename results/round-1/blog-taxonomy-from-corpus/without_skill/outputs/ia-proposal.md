# Blog categories and tags: a proposal

For the workshop facilitation and meetings blog: 24 posts, January to August 2025. Prepared 24 October 2026.

## Summary

- **Five categories, exactly one per post.** Four are in the main navigation: Meetings, Offsites & workshops, Activities, and People & dynamics. A fifth, For facilitators, holds the one post written for professional facilitators and sits in the footer only.
- **Thirteen tags instead of 26.** Each one has a written scope and belongs to one of four groups: setting, what you get, what it helps you do, and what's going wrong. Six old tags are retired outright: `tips`, `Tips`, `facilitation tips`, `misc`, `leadership` and `beginners`.
- **Every post is assigned** in the table below. There is also an old-to-new tag mapping with redirects, a short set of rules to stop the tags drifting again, and an appendix you can paste straight into front matter.

## What is wrong with the current tags

I counted every tag across all 24 posts.

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Too many tags, most of them used once | 26 distinct tags on 24 posts. 12 of them appear on a single post: retros, conflict, energy, hybrid, beginners, ideation, design sprint, tools, follow-up, risk, team health, business. Tags used once work like keywords, not as a way to browse. |
| "Tips" in three spellings | `tips` (2 posts), `Tips` (2), `facilitation tips` (4). Together they cover 7 posts, and the dominant-voices post carries two of them. Most static site generators treat `tips` and `Tips` as two separate tag pages. |
| "Tips" says nothing | The seven tips posts cover meetings, voting, brainstorming, conflict, airtime, first workshops and closing. Every post on the blog is a tip, so the tag can't tell one post from another. Merging the three spellings into one tag would not fix that. |
| `misc` hides two different problems | It is on 4 posts. Three of them are about meetings (the retro, hybrid fairness, meeting cost), so a meetings home was missing. The fourth (freelance pricing) is written for a different reader. |
| Meetings are under-tagged | Six posts are about team meetings, but only four carry `meetings`. The other two were tagged `misc`. |
| Some tags describe the reader, not the post | `leadership` (3 posts) and `beginners` (1) fit nearly every post, because nearly every reader is a team lead who is new to facilitation. |
| Different kinds of tag are mixed together | Content type (agendas), occasion (offsites, retros), audience (beginners, leadership), genre (tips) and topic (conflict, risk) all sit in one flat list. No tag reliably answers a question a reader would ask. |

## Who the structure is for

The readers are team leads who end up running their own meetings and offsites. They are not professional facilitators. They tend to arrive with one of three things in mind:

1. **An occasion.** "My weekly meeting is dull." "I have an offsite in three weeks."
2. **Something to do in the room.** "I need an icebreaker." "How do we vote on this without a row?"
3. **A problem with people.** "One person talks over everyone." "Two of them keep arguing." "Nobody speaks up."

The categories follow those three ways in. Two categories are occasions (Meetings, Offsites & workshops), one is things to do (Activities), and one is problems (People & dynamics). The labels use the reader's own words: meeting, offsite, activity. They avoid facilitator vocabulary such as formats, exercises, ideation and plenary.

### Sorting rules for this post and every future one

Apply these in order and stop at the first one that fits. The order is what settles the posts that could sit in two places.

1. Is it written for people who facilitate for a living, rather than for team leads? Put it in **For facilitators**.
2. Does it start from a problem with people, such as someone dominating, arguing, staying silent, or unclear decision ownership? Put it in **People & dynamics**.
3. Is it an activity, or a list of activities, that the group does together and that you could drop into any kind of session? Put it in **Activities**.
4. Is it about a recurring team meeting? Put it in **Meetings**.
5. Is it about planning or running a one-off, longer session? Put it in **Offsites & workshops**.

If nothing fits, that is a sign the post needs a new category or doesn't belong on the blog. It is never a reason to bring back `misc`.

### Why these categories and not another set

- **Sorting only by session type** (meetings, workshops, offsites) fails for half the blog. Icebreakers, dot voting and handling conflict work in every kind of session, so they would have no single home.
- **Sorting by phase** (before, during and after a session) is how facilitators think, not how team leads search. It would also split posts like the offsite agendas, which cover all three phases.
- **Sorting only by content type** (agendas, activities, guides) works for Activities, but it leaves "my weekly meeting is dull" and "one person dominates" with nowhere obvious to go. Content type survives as one tag, `agendas`, because "give me a ready-made agenda" is a real request.

The categories do mix kinds (two occasions, one content type, one problem type). That mix mirrors the three ways readers arrive, and the ordered rules above settle any overlap.

## Navigation

- **Main navigation:** Meetings, Offsites & workshops, Activities, People & dynamics, Start here. Meetings comes first because it is the occasion team leads face most often.
- **Start here page:** a short, hand-picked path for someone running their first session. It replaces the `beginners` tag. Suggested posts: Your first workshop: a checklist; A half-day strategy offsite agenda; 50 check-in questions; The parking lot; Handling the person who talks too much; Closing a workshop so things actually happen.
- **Two featured tags,** linked from the blog home and the category pages: Remote & hybrid, and Ready-made agendas. These are the two cross-cutting requests most likely to bring a team lead to the blog.
- **Footer:** For facilitators, plus a full tag index.
- **Each category page** opens with its one-line description from the categories table, so readers can tell at a glance whether they are in the right place.

## Rules to keep it clean

1. **Every post gets exactly one category.** There is no `misc`, "general" or "uncategorized" option.
2. **Every post gets one to three tags, taken only from the list.** Keep the list in one data file (see the appendix). If your generator allows it, make the build fail on any tag that isn't in that file. A check like that would have caught `tips` versus `Tips`.
3. **Write slugs in lowercase with hyphens, and set each display name once in the data file.** Never type a display name into a post.
4. **Never tag a post with its audience or its genre.** Words like "leadership", "beginners", "tips" and "how-to" apply to every post on this blog.
5. **Only add a new tag if all four of these hold:** you expect at least three posts on it within a year; it isn't a synonym of an existing tag; it isn't a category; and it uses the words a team lead would use.
6. **Review the tags once a year.** Fold any tag with fewer than three posts into its category. Split any tag that covers more than about a quarter of all posts, because it has stopped telling posts apart.

## Assumptions

I couldn't ask questions, so these are the calls I made. Each one is easy to reverse.

- **The 24 posts are the whole blog.** Each file holds a title and a one-paragraph summary, and I sorted the posts on those. If a full post goes somewhere its summary doesn't suggest, rerun the sorting rules on it.
- **The blog is built by a static site generator** (such as Jekyll, Hugo or Eleventy) that reads a `category` field and a `tags` list from front matter. The field names in the appendix may need adjusting for yours.
- **You want to keep the freelance pricing post.** It is written for a different reader, so it gets a small category that stays out of the main navigation. If you don't plan to write more for professional facilitators, consider moving it to an about or work-with-me page, or unpublishing it.
- **The labels come from judgment, not reader data.** I had no search or analytics data. The label I'm least sure of is "People & dynamics"; "Tricky moments" is the alternative. A quick, cheap check: give five team leads six tasks, such as "find help with one person dominating the meeting", and see which category they pick first.
- **Old tag pages may be linked from elsewhere,** which is why the mapping table above includes redirects.

## Gaps the new structure shows

These are not part of the reorganization, but sorting the posts made them visible:

- **People & dynamics is the thinnest main category (4 posts),** yet it covers some of the most common team-lead problems. Obvious next posts: the boss in the room, people on their phones, and the quiet expert.
- **Retrospectives has one post,** even though the retro is the ritual team leads run most often.
- **There is no ready-made agenda for a regular meeting** (weekly team meeting, standup) and **no remote offsite agenda.** Both would sit where the Meetings category and the `agendas` and `remote-and-hybrid` tags meet.
