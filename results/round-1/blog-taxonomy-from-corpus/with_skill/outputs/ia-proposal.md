# Blog reorganization: categories, tags and where every post goes

Draft for you to react to, 24 October 2026. Nothing in `posts/` has been changed.

## 1. Summary

Give every post exactly one of four categories, each named for what a team lead is trying to do: **Plan a meeting or offsite**, **Warm up and wrap up**, **Brainstorm, discuss and decide**, and **Handle tricky moments**. Replace the 26 free tags with a short controlled list that answers two separate questions: what kind of session it's for (Team meetings, Retros, Workshops, Offsites and planning days) and whether it works remotely (Remote and hybrid). Show each post's format (Agenda, Activity, Guide, List) as a badge rather than a tag.

The main reason is that your readers come with a job or a problem ("I need an offsite agenda", "one person talks over everyone"), not with a format or a topic in mind. When I applied this scheme to all 24 posts, every on-audience post found a home, the largest category held 29% of posts and none held fewer than four. Each alternative I tried either needed a catch-all bucket or used labels that describe the container instead of the need.

## 2. What I read, and who it seems to serve

The blog is 24 posts published between 1 January and 22 August 2025, about three a month. Each file holds front matter (title, date, two or three tags) and a one-paragraph body, so I classified each post from its title and that paragraph.

Nearly all of it is practical help you can use right away: agendas to copy, activities with steps, and what to do when a meeting goes sideways. It reads as written for someone who runs sessions now and then for their own team, which matches the team leads you describe. Two things don't fit that reading:

- **One post is written for professional facilitators:** "How to price a workshop as a freelance facilitator". Section 5.5 covers what to do with it.
- **Some vocabulary in the posts is facilitator jargon:** plenary, 1-2-4-All, fishbowl, RACI. Labels have to use plainer words than the posts do, or a team lead won't recognize them.

If this reading is wrong, the categories below are wrong too, so correct it first.

The problems, with counts:

1. **Three names for one vague idea.** `tips` (2), `Tips` (2) and `facilitation tips` (4) sit on 7 posts, and "Handling the person who talks too much" carries two of them. Under the new scheme those 7 posts land in all four categories, which shows the tag tells a reader nothing about what is inside.
2. **A junk drawer.** `misc` is on 4 posts (17%). Every one of them already has another tag, and the four end up in four different places.
3. **Dead-end tag pages.** 12 of the 26 tags (46%) are used once, so their tag page shows one post.
4. **Five dimensions mixed in one list.** Kinds of session (meetings, retros, offsites, planning, design sprint), formats (agendas, formats, exercises and the three tips variants), settings (remote, hybrid), topics (decision making, conflict, ideation, risk, energy, team health, follow-up, icebreakers, tools), audiences (leadership, beginners, business), plus `misc`. A reader scanning the tag list can't tell which question each tag answers.
5. **Overlapping terms.** `formats` and `exercises` mean the same thing ("Fishbowl discussions" carries both). `planning`, `offsites` and `agendas` overlap ("A two-day annual planning offsite" carries all three).
6. **A tag that is true of everything.** `leadership` is on 3 posts, but every post on this blog is for leaders.
7. **No single home for any post.** Without categories, the only way to browse is through 26 tag pages of uneven size.

There's one content finding as well, though it isn't a structure problem: the blog is thin where this audience spends its time. There is one post on retros and only two tagged offsites, although retros and offsites are two of the sessions a team lead most often has to run.

## 4. The options

I drafted three schemes and applied each to all 24 posts before choosing.

**A. By what you're trying to do (recommended for categories).** Plan a meeting or offsite / Warm up and wrap up / Brainstorm, discuss and decide / Handle tricky moments.
- *Serves:* someone arriving with a job or a problem.
- *Applied:* all 23 on-audience posts fit, at 7, 4, 7 and 5 posts. The largest category holds 29%, and no category is empty or holds a single post. Seven placements are borderline (section 5.4).
- *Costs:* labels are short phrases rather than single words. "Brainstorm, discuss and decide" will be the first category to need splitting as the blog grows.
- *Strains:* posts about habits rather than moments (psychological safety), and posts about setup rather than problems (hybrid meetings).

**B. By kind of session (kept as tags).** Team meetings / Retros / Workshops / Offsites and planning days.
- *Serves:* someone who thinks "I'm running a retro" or "I'm running our offsite". These are the strongest trigger words on the list.
- *Applied:* 14 posts belong to one kind of session. The other 9 on-audience posts (dot voting, silent brainstorming, conflict, the person who talks too much and others) work in any session, so they would need an "Any meeting" category holding 39% of posts. That is an "Other" bucket by another name. Retros would hold one post.
- *Verdict:* good tags, poor categories.

**C. By format (kept as a badge).** Agendas / Activities / Guides / Lists and checklists.
- *Serves:* readers who already know the shape they want ("give me an agenda").
- *Applied:* every post fits and no two terms overlap, but Guides holds 10 of 24 (42%, past the 40% balance limit).
- *Verdict:* the labels describe the container, not the need. A team lead whose colleague won't stop talking has no reason to click "Guides". Format works best as a cue on each post card, where people choose what to open.

**Also considered: by what the group needs to do** (warm up, generate ideas, discuss, decide, plan ahead, reflect, keep on track). That makes 7 categories for 23 posts, three of them with only one or two posts, and the preparation posts (checklist, tools, meeting cost) fit none. It is, however, the right way to split category 3 later (section 7).

**Recommendation:** A for categories, B for tags, C as a badge. Each field answers one question, so they combine without overlapping.

## 5. The proposal

### 5.1 Categories: exactly one per post

All four labels are task phrases in a team lead's words. One labelling system at one level.

| Category | Slug | What's in | What's out | Posts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plan a meeting or offsite** | `plan` | Everything before the day: agendas to copy, prep checklists, choosing tools, and whether the meeting should happen at all. Covers workshops too. | Activities you run in the room, even when an agenda includes them | 7 |
| **Warm up and wrap up** | `warm-up-and-wrap-up` | The edges of a session: icebreakers, check-in questions, energizers, and closings that turn talk into commitments and follow-up | Activities whose main job is the work itself | 4 |
| **Brainstorm, discuss and decide** | `brainstorm-discuss-decide` | Activities and formats for the main work: generating ideas, structured discussion (retros included), spotting risks, voting, and settling who decides | People problems | 7 |
| **Handle tricky moments** | `tricky-moments` | When the people side goes wrong: someone dominates, two people argue, the talk drifts, remote people get left out, people hold back | Techniques for the main work when nothing has gone wrong | 5 |

**Tie-break rule:** when a post fits two categories, file it under the problem the reader arrives with (usually the post's title), not the technique it uses.

### 5.2 Tags and format: the controlled lists

Each list is one dimension. Terms are lowercase slugs, which prevents another `Tips`/`tips` split.

**Session** (optional, up to two). Tag a session only when the post is written for that kind of session, not merely usable in it.

| Label | Slug | Definition | Search synonyms | Posts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Team meetings | `meetings` | The recurring meetings you hold with your own team: weekly, staff, standup | weekly meeting, staff meeting, status meeting, standup | 5 |
| Retros | `retros` | Looking back on a sprint, project or period to improve how the team works | retrospective, lessons learned, look-back | 1 |
| Workshops | `workshops` | A one-off working session to solve a specific problem or produce a specific deliverable | design sprint, working session | 5 |
| Offsites and planning days | `offsites` | Half a day or more spent setting direction, goals or plans, on site or away | off-site, retreat, away day, strategy day, planning session, OKRs, annual planning | 4 |

**Setting** (optional, one term).

| Label | Slug | Definition | Search synonyms | Posts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Remote and hybrid | `remote` | Written for sessions where some or all people join by video | virtual, online, Zoom, Teams, distributed, hybrid | 3 |

An in-person-only term isn't needed yet. Add one when five or more posts only work in a room. "Energizers" and "Fishbowl" are the current candidates.

**Format** (required, exactly one). Shown as a badge on every post card and at the top of every post.

| Label | Slug | Definition | Posts |
|---|---|---|---|

Guide is the default format and will stay the largest. That's acceptable because format is a badge, not a place people browse.

### 5.7 Navigation spec

- **Primary (header):** the four categories in this order: Plan a meeting or offsite, Warm up and wrap up, Brainstorm, discuss and decide, Handle tricky moments. Then Start here. That's five items. A new top-level item has to hold at least five posts and not overlap an existing category; anything smaller is a tag.
- **Utility:** search, plus subscribe and about if you have them.
- **Post index (`/blog/`):** four category doors, each with its label, its one-line description from 5.1 and a post count. These descriptions are the scent: a newcomer reads "icebreakers, check-in questions, energizers and closings" and knows where to click. Below the doors, a row of session chips (Team meetings, Retros, Workshops, Offsites and planning days) and a Remote and hybrid toggle. Then the latest posts.
- **Category hub:** label, description, then the posts with format badges, filterable by format and session. Hide any filter chip that would return zero posts.
- **Post page:** breadcrumb `Blog › Handle tricky moments › When two people won't stop arguing`. Show the format badge above the title and session chips below it. At the end, "More in Handle tricky moments" (three posts) and up to two hand-picked "Read next" links. Good pairs already in the corpus:
  - "Dot voting without the groupthink" and "Silent brainstorming beats shouting": both are about avoiding groupthink.
  - "Handling the person who talks too much", "When two people won't stop arguing" and "Psychological safety in five minutes a week".
  - "Your first workshop: a checklist" and "Closing a workshop so things actually happen".
  - "A half-day strategy offsite agenda", "A two-day annual planning offsite" and "Energizers for the post-lunch slump".
  - "Running Two Truths and a Lie on Zoom", "Making hybrid meetings fair to remote people" and "Choosing a remote whiteboard tool".
- **Footer:** the four categories, the four sessions, Remote and hybrid, Agendas, Activities and Start here.
- **Mobile:** search stays visible. The five header items collapse into a Browse menu that shows each category with its one-line description.
- **Active state:** the current category is highlighted in the header and named in the breadcrumb.

### 5.8 URL rules

- **Post URLs don't change.** Categories and tags stay out of post URLs, so you can recategorize any post later without breaking a link.
- **Categories** live at `/category/<slug>/` and **every other term** at `/tags/<slug>/`. Slugs are unique across all the lists, so they never collide.
- **Slugs are stable and separate from labels.** A label can be reworded (say "Handle tricky moments" to "When meetings go wrong") without the URL changing. Five old tag URLs survive this way with new labels: meetings, offsites, remote, agendas and retros.
- **Format:** lowercase kebab-case, trailing slash, enforced, with canonical tags. Mixed case (`/tags/Tips/`) redirects.
- **Query strings for filter state only:** for example `/category/plan/?format=agendas`.
- **Jekyll warning:** the filenames follow Jekyll's `_posts` convention. If that's your platform, its default permalink includes categories, so adding `category:` would move every post. Set an explicit permalink without `:categories` before adding the field, and check one post's URL before and after.

### 5.9 Front matter (the content model)

```yaml
---
title: "When two people won't stop arguing"
date: 2025-02-22
category: tricky-moments        # required, exactly one: plan | warm-up-and-wrap-up | brainstorm-discuss-decide | tricky-moments
format: guides                  # required, exactly one: agendas | activities | guides | lists
session: []                     # optional, up to two: meetings | retros | workshops | offsites
setting: []                     # optional: remote
listed: true                    # false = live at its URL, left out of indexes, tag pages and the feed
---
```

The old `tags:` line is removed once a post is migrated. Keep the allowed values in one data file (for example `taxonomy.yml`) and have the build fail on any value that isn't in it. That check is what stops terms from being created on the fly.

## 7. Governance and growth

- **Owner:** you. Every new term goes through you.
- **Adding a term:** check the synonym lists in 5.2 first. A new term needs at least three posts (published, or written within the quarter) and a definition that doesn't overlap an existing one. While publishing, never create a term: use the closest existing one and note the word you wanted in a "candidate terms" list in `taxonomy.yml`.
- **Term lifecycle:** proposed, active, deprecated (merged into another term, with a 301 and the posts re-tagged in the same change).
- **Quarterly drift audit.** Record these counts each time and compare. Today's baseline after migration: 4 categories (7, 4, 7, 5), 9 terms, 0 posts without a category, 1 unlisted post, 1 term with a single post (Retros), largest category 29%. Check for:
  - terms with no posts;
  - any category over 40% or over 15 posts;
  - near-duplicate candidate words;
  - posts whose terms no longer match their content;
  - words people search for that match no term or synonym.
- **Retros is on probation.** It holds one post. Write two more within the quarter, or fold it into Team meetings at the next audit. Keep it for now because "retro" is the word team leads search for.
- **Split plans, ready before they're needed:**
  - *Brainstorm, discuss and decide* splits first, into Brainstorm (silent brainstorming, premortem), Discuss (Lean Coffee, fishbowl, retros) and Decide (dot voting, decision rights).
  - *Warm up and wrap up* splits into Warm up (icebreakers, check-ins, energizers) and Wrap up and follow through (closings).
- **At ten times the content** (about 240 posts, or six to seven years at your current pace), the splits above produce eight or nine categories. Group them under Before (plan), In the room (warm up, brainstorm, discuss, decide, tricky moments) and After (follow through). That adds a second level only when the first gets too wide to scan.
- **Facets to add when the evidence appears:** group size (fishbowl, at 20 to 60 people, and Lean Coffee, at 4 to 12, already state one) once five posts depend on it; in-person only, as described in 5.2.

## 8. Assumptions and open questions

Assumptions I made because I couldn't ask:

1. **The audience is team leads** running their own team's meetings and offsites, not professional facilitators (from your brief). The labels are written in their words.
2. **The 24 files are the whole blog,** and the one-paragraph bodies represent the posts well enough to classify them.
3. **The platform is a static site** built from Markdown front matter, with tag pages at `/tags/<tag>/` and the index at `/blog/`, and post URLs that don't contain tags.
4. **You are the only author** and the only person approving terms.
5. **No analytics, site-search logs or reader questions** were available. Those are the cheapest test of whether the labels match what people type.
6. **The blog keeps its current mix and pace,** about three posts a month.

Questions that would change the proposal:

- Do you want to serve freelance or aspiring facilitators? The answer decides what happens to the pricing post and whether a fifth category is coming.
- Which platform is this? That decides how to implement `listed: false`, the extra fields and the permalink setting.
- Do you have search logs or analytics? The top searches would show whether "Warm up and wrap up" and "Brainstorm, discuss and decide" are the right words.
- Is your next writing on offsites and retros? Those are the thinnest areas relative to what your readers run.

## 9. How to validate it

These are research designs, not results. Nothing below has been run.

**Optional first step (cheap): hybrid card sort.** Recruit 10 to 15 readers who say they are team leads, for example through your newsletter. Give them the 23 on-audience titles and the four category labels, and let them add their own groups. If they create a group you don't have, or rename one of yours, use their word. If a title scatters across groups, it needs a cross-link, or the categories are cut the wrong way.

**Tree test (before you rebuild the navigation).** Use 20 to 30 team leads, not facilitators. The tree is text only: the four categories, with each category's posts listed under it exactly as in section 5.3. A task succeeds when the participant picks the right post. Directness is the share who choose the right category on the first click.

| # | Task (in the reader's words, avoiding the labels) | Correct answer | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your team is spending a day away from the office next month to set priorities. You need to work out what happens hour by hour. | Plan › A half-day strategy offsite agenda, or A two-day annual planning offsite | 80% |
| 2 | You want a quick game to get people talking at the start of Monday's video call. | Warm up and wrap up › Running Two Truths and a Lie on Zoom | 80% |
| 3 | One person on your team answers every question before anyone else can speak. | Handle tricky moments › Handling the person who talks too much | 80% |
| 4 | You've been asked to run a half-day session for the first time and want to know what to get ready. | Plan › Your first workshop: a checklist | 80% |
| 5 | Your team has ten ideas on the board and needs to narrow them to two without the loudest voice winning. | Brainstorm, discuss and decide › Dot voting without the groupthink | 70% |
| 6 | Your team's end-of-sprint look-back has turned into a box-ticking exercise. | Brainstorm, discuss and decide › Why your retro feels stale | 70% |
| 7 | Half the team dials in from home and keeps getting talked over by the people in the room. | Handle tricky moments › Making hybrid meetings fair to remote people | 70% |
| 8 | Your meetings end with lots of good ideas, but two weeks later nobody has done anything. | Warm up and wrap up › Closing a workshop so things actually happen | 70% |
| 9 | Your manager asks whether the weekly status meeting is worth an hour of everyone's time. | Plan › What your weekly meeting really costs | 70% |
| 10 | Your team keeps clashing over who gets the final say on hiring. | Brainstorm, discuss and decide › Who decides? Using a simple decision rights grid | 70% |
| 11 | People on your team rarely disagree with you in meetings, and you suspect they are holding back. | Handle tricky moments › Psychological safety in five minutes a week | 70% |

Tasks 1 to 4 are top tasks, so they need 80%. Tasks 6 to 11 test the borderline placements in 5.4. When one of those fails, look at where people clicked first. If most chose a single other category, move the post there. If clicks scattered, the post's title is the problem, not its category. If a top task fails, reword the category label and test that task again with new participants.
