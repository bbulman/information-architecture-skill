# Blog reorganization proposal

A draft for you to react to. The post-by-post placement is in Appendix A and the old-tag mapping is in Appendix B.

## 1. Summary

Organize the blog by the job a team lead is trying to get done: five categories named as tasks, plus ten controlled tags that replace the current 26. Your readers arrive with a situation ("I have an offsite to run", "one person won't stop talking"), not with a content type in mind. I applied the draft to all 24 posts: 23 fit, five of those less cleanly, and one (the freelance pricing post) is written for a different reader, so it has a least-bad home and a flag.

| Category | What a reader finds there | Posts |
|---|---|---|
| Plan a workshop or offsite | Ready-made agendas, prep checklists and how to close well | 8 |
| Run better meetings | Fixing recurring meetings, retros and hybrid calls | 5 |
| Warm up the group | Icebreakers, energizers and check-in questions | 3 |
| Get input and make decisions | Brainstorming, voting and working out who decides | 5 |
| Handle tough team dynamics | Arguments, people who talk too much, people who won't speak up | 3 |

**Tags:** Agendas, Exercises, Offsites, Remote and hybrid, Strategy and planning, Prep and follow-up, Decisions, Getting everyone heard, Keeping on track, Leadership.

**How I read the blog** (correct me if this is wrong, because everything below builds on it): 24 short how-to posts dated January to August 2025, about meetings, workshops and offsites, for team leads who run their own sessions rather than professional facilitators. I have seen no analytics, search logs or reader questions, so nothing here rests on usage data.

The problems, in order of how much they hurt a reader:

- **Twelve of the 26 tags are used once.** Each one is a dead-end page holding a single post.
- **The tips family spans 7 posts under three spellings**, and "Handling the person who talks too much" carries two of them at once. Merging them into one tag would not help. Nearly every post on this blog is a tip, so the tag can't separate anything.
- **misc is on 4 posts**, and each has a real subject: a retro, hybrid meetings, meeting cost, freelance pricing.
- **Four kinds of thing are mixed in one list:** format, setting, topic and audience. A reader can't tell what a tag page is promising.
- **The form is inconsistent:** capitals (Tips), spaces (decision making, design sprint, facilitation tips, team health) and a hyphen (follow-up).

**Gaps (my reading of the posts, not data):** you named offsites as one of the two things your readers run, yet only 2 of 24 posts are about offsites specifically, and neither summary mentions getting people there, the venue or the budget. Retros, probably a regular job for this reader, have one post.

## 3. The options

I drafted three schemes and sorted all 24 posts into each.

| | A. By the reader's job (recommended) | B. By type of content | C. By kind of session |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Categories** | The five in section 1 | Agendas, Icebreakers, Exercises, Tools and checklists, Advice | Team meetings, Workshops, Offsites, Remote and hybrid, Any session |
| **Who it serves** | A lead with a problem or an event coming up | A returning reader who already knows they want an agenda or a game | A reader who starts from "I have an offsite" |
| **Posts per category** | 8, 5, 3, 5, 3 | 4, 3, 6, 3, 8 | 5, 5, 2, 3, 9 |
| **Where it strains** | Five posts sit between two jobs, and one post fits no job | "Advice" (8 posts) is the old tips tag under a new name, and a reader must know the format before they can find the fix | "Any session" (9 posts, 38%) is misc by another name, because dot voting or a dominant talker belong to every kind of session |
| **What it costs** | A fresh decision on every post (done in Appendix A) | Lowest: most existing tags map straight across | Moderate |

I recommend A because it is the only scheme with no catch-all bucket and the only one that starts from what the reader is trying to do. B and C are not wasted: format and setting are real ways to slice this content, so they return as tags (Agendas, Exercises, Offsites, Remote and hybrid). Each post has one category, and tags handle the cross-cuts.

## 4. The proposal

### 4.1 Categories

Each post gets exactly one. All five labels are tasks, so a reader scans one kind of label. None uses the word "facilitation", because your readers don't call themselves facilitators.

| Label | Slug | What goes here | What doesn't |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan a workshop or offsite | `plan-workshop-offsite` | Whole sessions longer than a normal meeting: agendas, prep, tools chosen in advance, closing and follow-up | A single activity you could drop into any session |
| Run better meetings | `better-meetings` | Recurring team meetings: structure, retros, staying on track, fairness, whether the meeting should exist | One-off special sessions |
| Warm up the group | `warm-up` | Icebreakers, energizers and check-ins, whose point is to get people ready | Activities meant to produce an output |
| Get input and make decisions | `input-and-decisions` | Activities and frameworks that draw out ideas, views or risks, or settle who decides and what | Problems caused by how people are behaving |
| Handle tough team dynamics | `team-dynamics` | Arguments, someone dominating, people afraid to speak up | Activities chosen for their output |

**Tie-breaker:** ask what the reader was doing when they went looking for this post. If there are two answers, use the job named in the post's title.

"Getting everyone heard" and "Keeping on track" are new terms I built from words in your posts ("airtime", "speak up", "on track"); they are the two labels most worth checking with readers.

### 4.3 Tagging rules

1. Every post has exactly one category and one to three tags. The one exception is the freelance pricing post, which has none until you decide its future.
2. Tag a post's main point, not a passing mention.
3. Slugs are lowercase and hyphenated. Display labels are sentence case.
4. A tag must apply to at least two posts, and must never hold exactly the same posts as a category.
5. No new tags while publishing. See section 8.

**Start here** replaces the old `beginners` tag. Since your whole audience is non-specialist, a tag would catch either one post or all of them. My suggested links, chosen from the content and not from traffic, are the first-workshop checklist, the parking lot, check-in questions, dot voting, the person who talks too much, the half-day offsite agenda and closing a workshop. It only links to them: each post's one home is still its category.

**At ten times the size** (about 240 posts) the five categories should hold, at roughly 48 posts each, but category pages will need tag filters and a "time needed" field becomes worth adding. Only 7 of the 24 summaries state a length today, so I left it out.

## 8. Keeping it tidy

- **Owner:** you.
- **One vocabulary file** (for example `taxonomy.yml`) holding the tables in 4.1 and 4.2, starting at version 1.0, with a short change log.
- **Adding a term:** check the synonyms first, confirm it applies to at least two posts today, add it to the file, then use it. If your generator allows it, a build check that fails on any tag not in the file stops drift at the source.
- **Term lifecycle:** proposed, active, then deprecated and merged into another term. Deprecated terms keep their redirects.
- **Watch list:** retros, conflict and design sprints have one post each. Propose them as tags when the second post exists.
- **Quarterly drift check** (about nine new posts at your pace): tags with no posts; any term over 40% of posts; near-duplicates; posts with no tags (only the freelance post expected); tags that no longer match their posts; search queries with no matching term, if you have logs. Record the counts so the next check can compare.
