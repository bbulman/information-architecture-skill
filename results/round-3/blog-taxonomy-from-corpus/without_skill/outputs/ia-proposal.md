# Blog reorganization proposal

**Recommendation:** replace your 26 tags with 4 categories and 7 tags, organized around what a team lead is trying to run or fix rather than what kind of post you wrote. 23 of the 24 posts get a clear home. The last one, on freelance pricing, is not written for your readers, so I recommend keeping it live but out of the browsing paths.

**One decision for you:** whether that freelance post stays. I have assumed it does; the details are under [Outside the categories](#outside-the-categories-1).

## What is wrong today

Your 24 posts carry 26 different tags, used 51 times. Twelve of those tags appear on a single post, so clicking one leads nowhere. There are four separate problems:

- **Tags that say nothing.** `misc`, `tips`, `Tips`, `facilitation tips` and `leadership` could go on almost anything.
- **Duplicates.** `tips` and `Tips`, `remote` and `hybrid`, `formats` and `exercises`.
- **Writers' words.** `ideation`, `formats` and `facilitation tips` are how a professional describes the work. A team lead does not think of themselves as a facilitator.
- **Mixed kinds of label.** One list holds content types (`agendas`), topics (`risk`), settings (`remote`) and audiences (`beginners`), so a reader cannot scan it for anything.

Your `tips` problem is bigger than a spelling cleanup. The three variants cover seven posts, and merging them into one tag would only give you a tidier version of a label that does not help anyone choose. They should go. Likewise, three of the four `misc` posts are about team meetings, so `misc` was hiding a category your readers need.

## Categories

Every post gets exactly one. They are listed in navigation order.

| Category | What the reader is thinking | Intro line for the category page | Posts |

| **Team Meetings** | "The meetings we already have aren't working." | Fix the meetings you already hold: retros, hybrid calls, and the ones you should cancel. | 5 |
| **Offsites & Workshops** | "I have to run a big session and I need a plan." | Plan and run the one-off session: agendas, prep, tools and what happens afterwards. | 7 |
| **Activities** | "I need something to do with the group for the next 20 minutes." | Icebreakers, voting, brainstorming and other exercises to drop into any session. | 7 |
| **Team Dynamics** | "The agenda is fine. The problem is what happens between people." | Arguments, over-talkers, silence, and not knowing who decides. | 4 |

**How to sort a post.** Ask these in order and stop at the first yes:

1. Does it take its own slot on an agenda, inside a longer session? **Activities.**
2. Is it about how people behave or who holds authority, whatever the session? **Team Dynamics.**
3. Is it about a meeting the team holds regularly? **Team Meetings.**
4. Is it about planning or running a one-off session? **Offsites & Workshops.**
5. None of these? Do not create a catch-all. It is either off-topic for your readers or the first post in a future category.

**Why this shape.** A team lead arrives with a situation: an offsite coming up, a meeting that has gone flat, one person who will not stop talking. These four categories match those situations, in words they already use.

**What I rejected:**

- **Sorting by content type (Tips, Agendas, Exercises).** This is where your current tags were drifting. It answers "what did you write?" rather than "what do I need?", and almost everything would land in Tips.
- **A Remote category.** Only three posts, and being remote cuts across everything: a remote icebreaker is still an icebreaker. It works better as a tag.
- **Before, during and after a session.** Tidy for a professional facilitator, but a team lead thinks about the event in front of them, not the phase of it.

## Rules to keep it clean

1. One category per post.
2. Zero to three tags per post, only from the list above, always lowercase and hyphenated.
3. A new tag needs three posts that would use it. `retros`, `brainstorming`, `follow-through` and `tools` are next in line.
4. No tag that describes the whole blog. That rules out `tips`, `misc`, `leadership` and their relatives.
5. A new category needs about five posts that fail the sorting questions. Split Activities into Icebreakers and Exercises once it passes about 15 posts.

If your blog has a build step, make it fail on any tag not on the list. That is the cheapest way to stop `Tips` coming back.

## How to apply it

Update each post's front matter from the tables above. For example:

```yaml
---
title: "Dot voting without the groupthink"
date: 2025-02-01
category: activities
tags: [decisions, getting-everyone-heard]
---
```

Category values are `team-meetings`, `offsites-workshops`, `activities` and `team-dynamics`.

Keep post URLs as they are, and do not put the category in the URL, so filing a post somewhere else later never breaks a link. If your old tag pages are public, redirect `meetings`, `offsites` and `exercises` to their new category pages, `remote` and `hybrid` to `remote-hybrid`, `decision-making` to `decisions`, and the rest to the blog home.
